# Scientific Research

## Use When

The goal is to understand a field, synthesize evidence, compare competing models, or prepare for informed engagement with domain experts.

## Don't Use When

- The real task is market research or product positioning
- The user wants a fast summary rather than a structured evidence review

## Inputs

- Research question
- Starting knowledge level
- Relevant subfields or populations
- Required level of rigor

## Typical Pass Sequence

1. Project initiation
2. Field map
3. Core evidence review
4. Competing models
5. Methods and limitations
6. Reading list and gap analysis
7. Synthesis

## Outputs

- Field map
- Core evidence memo
- Competing models comparison
- Methods/limitations note
- Reading list
- Synthesis document with confidence levels

## Quality Checks

- Major claims are backed by reviews, meta-analyses, or primary literature
- Evidence strength is differentiated from popularity of a theory
- Methods and limitations are surfaced rather than buried
- Remaining open questions are explicit

## Failure Modes

- Narrative bias toward the most elegant theory
- Authority bias toward famous names or institutions
- Citation hallucination or weak source verification
- Collapsing disagreement into fake consensus
