# Market Research

## Use When

The goal is to understand a market, category, competitor set, user pain, business model landscape, or product positioning question.

## Don't Use When

- The real task is scientific literature review or evidence synthesis
- The user only needs a quick competitor list, not a structured assessment

## Inputs

- Product or concept under consideration
- Geography and segment boundaries
- Known competitors or adjacent categories
- Decision to support (go/no-go, positioning, prioritization, build scope)

## Typical Pass Sequence

1. Project initiation
2. Landscape survey
3. User voice / complaints / unmet needs
4. Competitive deep-dive
5. Market structure and barriers
6. Positioning and feasibility
7. Synthesis / decision

## Outputs

- Landscape map
- User voice memo
- Competitor comparison
- Market structure memo
- Positioning or decision document

## Quality Checks

- Real users are represented, not just company marketing copy
- Competitor comparisons use consistent dimensions
- Claims about market size or growth are sourced
- Positioning is grounded in actual gaps, not wishful thinking

## Failure Modes

- Source monoculture (all sources are vendor pages)
- Confirmation bias toward a desired product idea
- Confusing product desirability with market viability
- No distinction between user complaints and fatal market gaps
