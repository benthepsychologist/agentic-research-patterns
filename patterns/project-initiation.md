# Project Initiation

## Use When

The user has a topic, problem, idea, or question but has not yet turned it into a scoped research effort.

## Don't Use When

- The project is already well-scoped and the next step is an actual research pass.
- The user wants a quick answer rather than a structured investigation.

## Inputs

- Topic or thesis
- Starting context and background knowledge
- Stakes, constraints, and deadlines
- Suspected blind spots or uncertainties

## Pass Sequence

1. Clarify the question
2. Reflect existing knowledge back to the user
3. Convert vague curiosity into specific research questions
4. Define scope and out-of-scope boundaries
5. Design the pass sequence
6. Define success criteria and likely biases

## Outputs

- Initiation document
- Research plan
- Initial success criteria

## Quality Checks

- Questions are specific and answerable
- The pass plan is finite and sequenced logically
- Out-of-scope boundaries are explicit
- Success criteria are concrete enough to know when to stop

## Failure Modes

- Scope remains too broad
- Questions are performative rather than decision-relevant
- Research plan skips from topic to conclusion without intermediate passes
- No stopping condition is defined
