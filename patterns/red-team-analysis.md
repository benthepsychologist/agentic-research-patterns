# Red-Team Analysis

## Use When

The goal is to stress-test an idea, thesis, strategy, argument, or product concept before commitment.

## Don't Use When

- The user wants neutral exploration rather than adversarial critique
- The position under review is still too vague to attack coherently

## Inputs

- The position under review
- The affirmative case for it
- Stakes and costs of being wrong
- Any known fragile assumptions

## Typical Pass Sequence

1. Steel-man the strongest counterarguments
2. Audit assumptions
3. Map likely failure modes
4. Identify alternatives or hybrids
5. Produce an adversarial synthesis / verdict

## Outputs

- Counterarguments memo
- Assumption audit
- Failure mode register
- Alternatives memo
- Red-team verdict

## Quality Checks

- Counterarguments are strong enough to make the owner uncomfortable
- Assumptions are explicit and falsifiable
- Failure modes include likelihood and severity, not just drama
- Final verdict distinguishes proceed / modify / abandon / research more

## Failure Modes

- Straw-manning the critique
- Blurring critique and decision into one pass
- Softening attacks because the original idea feels attractive
- Ignoring second-order effects
